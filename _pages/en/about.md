I am a doctoral student in the Graduate School of Interdisciplinary Information Studies at **the University of Tokyo**, advised by [Prof. Jun Rekimoto](https://lab.rekimoto.org/). My research in **Human–Computer Interaction (HCI)** focuses on wearable devices and speech interfaces that help people communicate when speaking aloud is difficult or their surroundings are noisy.

This work began with a question during my commute: could I make a phone call on a train without disturbing others? During my master’s studies, I developed mask-based interfaces that infer speech from mouth movements. I now work across **sensor design and machine learning**, from recognizing extremely quiet whispers with [SilentWhisper](https://doi.org/10.1109/ACCESS.2025.3612004) to capturing speech in noisy environments with [ClothTalk](https://doi.org/10.1109/ACCESS.2025.3631752) and [MaskClip](https://lab.rekimoto.org/projects/maskclip/).

My goal is to **turn research into tools people can use in everyday life**. I bring together hardware, software, design, and prototyping, and value opportunities to share working prototypes with people beyond academia at Maker Faire and other exhibitions. Outside research, I enjoy ball juggling.

I have been an Individual Researcher in **JST ACT-X** since 2023, continuing into its Acceleration Phase in April 2026. Since December 2025, I have also been a **Research Scholar (Internship) at the University of Washington**, Paul G. Allen School of Computer Science & Engineering.
